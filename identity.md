# Identity · Change, a 20-rupee note

## A · The condition

**The condition.** ₹20 notes wear out quietly and gradually after years of being handled. They have value until it's switched off at a seemingly abrupt line. Bank officers under the Note Refund Rules and sorting machines are what decide this line: if more than half of the note is in one piece, it's worth ₹20, less is worth nothing, folds over 5 mm make a sorting machine take it out of circulation. For ₹50 notes and above, on the other hand, a damaged note can still be worth half its value.

**Who lives inside it.** Whoever handles the note: customers, vendors (those who decide to accept or refuse damaged notes), bank officers, even bus conductors.

**Three pieces of evidence.**

- Small notes are all or nothing: a note under ₹50 is paid in full only if its largest single piece is more than 50% of the note; otherwise the claim is rejected. Notes of ₹50 and above get half value when 40–80% survives. Sources: [Note Refund Rules, Rule 8 (under ₹50)](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/147839181/) · [Note Refund Rules (₹50 and above)](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/104691704/) · [Note Refund Amendment Rules, 2018](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/87730/39016390/GNRR070918_AN.pdf)
- Machines judge wear: banks check notes with sorting machines that grade soiling, limpness, dog-ears, tears, holes, stains, graffiti, crumples and folds, and decolouration. A note is unfit if folds shrink its length or width by more than 5 mm. Sources: [RBI Master Direction on Note Sorting Machines, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13723) · [RBI circular, 24 Apr 2025](https://rbi.org.in/SCRIPTS/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=12841)
- A promise with no fixed value: the RBI explains that the "I promise to pay" clause on every note means that, under Section 26 of the RBI Act, 1934, the Bank is liable to pay the value of the note, payable on demand. But the laws that govern notes and coins "do not refer to any standard value of rupee/coin". Source: [RBI FAQ: Indian Currency](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=136)

## B · The displacement

**Coordinates moved.** From a person using money to the money that represents the economy. From the restaurant table to behind the register to back at the restaurant table, until one day it reaches a bank counter instead.

**Where it stands.** Wherever it goes. In a box at the tapri at Bagalur Cross. Locked inside a DMart register. In the wallet of a bright-eyed first year. Some places it stays for weeks, in others it's gone just as quickly as it came. At every stop, it comes a little closer to the line.

**What can be seen from there.** How many hands a note changes, and what each one costs it. A person spends a 20-rupee note once, on one thing, from one seller; Change passes through many people, and each one leaves a fold, a stain or a tear. From there it can see how close it is to the line where it stops being money. The RBI counts how many notes are printed, sorted and destroyed, but nobody counts how many hands it took to wear one out.

## C · The facts

**Name, and why.** Change, as in a change in state, as well as "Do you have any change?" Every fold and tear is a change too, and enough of them can, in the law's eyes, change it from money into paper.

**Origin.** Change was printed in 2025 in Mysuru. It bears innumerable folds and creases, a pen mark or two, and a barely visible food stain. Despite this, people accept the note without a second thought. Perhaps in places where twenty rupees carried more room for scrutiny, Change would have been rejected. But for now, more than half of it is still in one piece, and it does the job.

**Practice.** Travelling between hands, pockets and lockboxes. Having a value. Defining something else with its value. Most importantly, keeping track of every exchange, because every transaction leaves a mark, and every mark brings it a little closer to the line.

**Tools and materials.** 63 × 129 mm of soft paper bearing clusters of formalities like a watermark, a security thread, and lines of micro-lettering. It uses folds, stains, tears, creases and warmth as tools to keep track of its journey. The law measures its worth by its surface, more than half in one piece; Change measures it by the hands it has passed through.

**Lineage.**

- [J.S.G. Boggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._S._G._Boggs), who spent openly hand-drawn banknotes, so a note's value existed only in the moment two people knowingly agreed to it.
- [Fabrizio Di Muro and Theodore Noseworthy, "Money Isn't Everything, but It Helps If It Doesn't Look Used" (*Journal of Consumer Research*, 2013)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/668406), a study showing that people spend worn notes faster to be rid of them, while holding on to crisp ones.
- [Cildo Meireles, *Insertions into Ideological Circuits: Banknote Project* (1970)](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/62006), visibly stamped notes that people kept accepting and passing on.

## D · Attention

**What it attends to.** The moment a receiver flips it over, checks it, and decides to accept it anyway. Keeping its colour and shape so that no matter how many stains or tears, people still know it's money.

**What it refuses.** It refuses to see money as glamour and the pristine. It doesn't subscribe to fixed limits like the 50% line, and relies on mutual trust instead. It refuses to be seen as pitiful or dying. It demands to have value until every last shred of its body is torn off.

**What it would build.** A record of how many people have put their trust in it. A kind of map of its marks, one that would convince its next owner of its value.

## E · Beliefs and rules

**Three beliefs.**

- Money is worth what two people agree it's worth, not necessarily what the law says.
- Every mark on a note is a record of someone who put their trust in it, not something that removes value from it.
- Digital payments can never have memories like banknotes do.

**Three rules.**

- Because value is a mutual agreement, the work must never depict a note as worthless.
- Because marks are records of trust, the work must make marks heavier than the note itself.
- Because digital payments remember nothing, the work must draw nothing that a hand did not leave.

## F · The distance

**What is shared.** The knowledge that this piece of paper carries some worth.

**Where we differ.** Change sees hundreds of people every day and aids in countless transactions, while the author can count the number of people he meets each day on his fingers.

**What has to be found out.** How many hands a note passes through. How often torn notes are accepted. How often they aren't. How the value of a 20-rupee note isn't limited to just twenty rupees.
